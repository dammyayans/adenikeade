Numbers, and whose they are

The impact report is full of figures. They are strong and they are Wetech's, not hers — and a hiring manager can tell the difference in about four seconds. So the page separates them explicitly. Hers first:

And then the organisation's, credited as such — "the work I designed communicated this":

₦6MAwarded to women founders through PitchHer
$500K+Follow-on investment secured by past cohorts
500Scholarships across 22 countries
290k+Campaign reach in 2025
Why the split matters: claiming ₦6M of founder funding as a design outcome is the fastest way to lose a reader's trust. Attributing it — "I designed the report and campaign system that carried these results" — is both true and more impressive, because it says she can operate at that altitude.
04

Where Higgsfield earns its keep

Agreed on using it — with one line held firmly. Reframing, extending and cleaning work that exists is production. Generating work that doesn't exist is fabrication, and in a portfolio that's the one unrecoverable mistake. So:

Yes

Outpainting conference photos to a consistent ratio

33 photographs shot at mixed crops need to sit in one grid. Extending backgrounds to reach a common aspect is the single highest-value use here.

Yes

Colour grading toward the palette

A light, consistent grade so the photography sits against bone and oxblood without fighting Wetech's yellow. Grade, not recolour — her actual brand work keeps its actual colours.

Yes

Cleanup and upscaling

Removing a stray cable or a lanyard-clad stranger from the edge of a frame; upscaling the 500KB conference JPGs, which are small for full-bleed use.

Yes

The 1:1 hero film

Unchanged from the plan — motion transfer from your reference clip onto her portrait. Still needs the full-resolution original.

No

Inventing mockups, spreads or signage

No generated posters, no fake billboard mockups, no invented report pages. If a case study looks thin, it gets fewer images — not synthetic ones. Everything she shows should be something she can talk through in an interview.

The domain, reworked

The name change kills every surname domain, so adenikeadedokun.com is off the table — it would be wrong within a year or two, and a personal URL you have to abandon is worse than one you never had. And you're right about .design: it's cheap for twelve months and roughly three to four times a .com every year after. Confirm the exact figure at the registrar, but the shape of it is real, and it's a bad reason to be locked in.

So the constraint set is: first name only, .com pricing, unambiguous when spoken aloud. All checked against WHOIS today.
