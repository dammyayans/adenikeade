Four candidate marks for Adenike Adedokun, drawn in vector from the same typeface the site uses. Plus the domain call. The hero now carries her full name — so the mark's job is everything the name is too big for.

01

What the mark has to do

The hero is her full name at 180px, so the logo is never the biggest thing on the page. That frees it to be a small, hard-working object instead of a statement. Five real jobs:

01

Favicon · 16px

The hardest constraint. Anything with more than three visual elements turns to mush at browser-tab size.

02

Nav lockup · 24px

Top-left of every page, sits beside the live Lagos clock. Must read on bone, oxblood and pink grounds.

03

Case-study header · 40px

Separate case pages need a persistent home link. This is where it earns its keep.

04

Avatar

Behance, LinkedIn, Gmail. A square crop, no wordmark, high contrast at 48px.

05

Film watermark

Bottom corner of the 1:1 hero loop. Small, monochrome, must not fight her face.

Why these are vector and not AI-generated: an image model gives you a picture of a logo — soft edges, no outlines, no way to recolour a stroke or set it at 16px without it falling apart. These are drawn in SVG from Bricolage Grotesque, the same face the site sets. They're production files from the moment you pick one.
02

Four marks

M1

AdeA

Your idea, executed

Exactly what you asked for. First three letters of the first name, capital A returning at the end for the surname — so it reads Adenike Adedokun without spelling either out. The final A carries the pink; that's the accent's one appearance in the identity.

Weakness: at 16px "AdeA" is four letterforms in a row and turns into a grey smudge. It's a wordmark, not a favicon — it needs a companion symbol.

M2

The Double-A

The monogram

Two A's sharing one leg. Her initials become a single continuous zigzag — which is also, not accidentally, the shape of a stitch. One crossbar is pink, the other is ink, so the two letters stay legible as two letters rather than collapsing into a W.

Strong at every size and completely unambiguous as a monogram. It is also the most conventional of the four — a lot of designers land on an initials ligature.

M3

The Granny Square

Recommended symbol

No letters at all. This is the actual construction of a crochet granny square — concentric rounds worked outward from a centre ring — turned 45° and drawn in three colours from her cardigan. It's the same idea the whole site is built on, compressed into one object.

It is the only one of the four that says brand designer with a point of view rather than person with initials. It reads at 16px because it's three nested shapes, not three letters. And it works as a monochrome outline for the film watermark.

M4

The full wordmark

Recommended primary

Her name, set once, tight, with a small registered mark riding high on the last letter. Not a logo so much as a considered setting of the name — which for a brand designer is the more confident move. It's what goes in the nav, the footer, the case-study headers and the résumé.

The ® is a deliberate borrow from the reference set: it signals "this is a brand, not a freelancer's homepage" and costs one glyph.

03

The system I'd ship

Not one mark — a pair. The wordmark carries the name everywhere there's room; the granny square handles every square, small or monochrome context. That's the standard structure and it means neither has to compromise for the other.

Horizontal lockup

Square + wordmark, baseline-aligned, one square-width of clear space between. Nav, footer, case headers, email signature, the résumé PDF header.

Square avatar · favicon · watermark

Three approved fills: full-colour on oxblood, reversed on pink, single-colour on bone. Never on a photograph without a solid tile behind it.

Where AdeA goes: not dead. It becomes the shorthand — the URL slug, the Instagram handle, the file-naming prefix, the loading state. It just isn't the logo, because a four-letter wordmark can't do the 16px job and the square can.
04

The domain

Superseded by asset map 003. This table assumed the surname was permanent. It isn't — Adenike Adedokun becomes Adenike Ayanrinde, which rules out every surname domain below, and .design renews at roughly three to four times a .com after the first year. The live recommendation is byadenike.com. Kept here as the record of what was considered.
DomainStatusHow it reads
adenike.design Available My recommendation. Short, unambiguous, says the profession in the URL itself. "adenike dot design" is one clean phrase out loud — the test that matters when she says it in an interview. .design is well-established and reads as deliberate rather than as a .com she couldn't get.
adenikeadedokun.com Available Buy this too, and 301 it to the primary. It's what someone types after reading her name on a CV or a LinkedIn profile, and it costs about $12/year to not lose that traffic.
adenikea.com Available Available, but I'd argue against it. Run together it breaks in the wrong place — most people read adenik-ea or hear an echo of IKEA — and it drops the surname without gaining any brevity over adenike.design. Hard to dictate over a phone.
adenikeade.com Available Same problem, less recognisable. Skip.
ade-a.com Available Hyphens read as a compromise, always. Skip.
adenike.studio Unverified Good fallback if .design is gone by the time you buy. Slight mismatch though — she's an in-house brand designer, not a studio, and the TLD makes a claim the site doesn't back up yet.
adenike.works Unverified Nice double meaning with a "Selected Work" section, but .works still reads as novelty to a lot of recruiters. Third choice.
Buy two, use one: adenike.design as canonical, adenikeadedokun.com redirecting to it. Set the canonical tag to the .design so search never splits between them. Email on the same domain — hello@adenike.design — is worth far more than a Gmail address on the contact section.
