01

The read

Adenike is not a generalist designer with a pink preference. She is a brand designer — identity systems, event worlds, campaign assets at volume — currently holding two roles at once: Brand Designer at Interswitch and Lead Brand Designer at Wetech. The portfolio's job is to convert a hiring manager or a founder in under forty seconds.

That points the content at outcomes, not process diagrams. She has real numbers and almost nobody at her level does. They go above the fold, not in a footer.

The risk to avoid: a soft, pretty, "girly-creative" page that reads as junior. The colour stays; the typography and the restraint around it are what make it read senior. One loud thing, everything else quiet.

06

Motion menu

Pick three. Not eight — stacked effects read as a demo reel and cost more in Usability (30% of the score) than they earn in Creativity (20%). Shaded cells are my picks for Direction A.

Pick · Hook

Section ground crossfade

Body background transitions between palette colours as sections enter. Cheap, unusual on a light site, and it is the whole idea of Direction A.

Pick

Cursor-follow project label

Hovering a project spawns a pill that trails the cursor reading View — Wetech. Has a real mobile equivalent: the label is always visible on touch.

Pick

Staggered entrance

Logo 0s → nav .15s → eyebrow .3s → display type .45s → CTA .6s. One spring curve everywhere: cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1).

Hold

Cursor-scrubbed film

Mouse X scrubs the hero video. Spectacular, but it's Direction B's hook — using it in A would be a second hook competing with the stripe.

Hold

Diagonal marquee tapes

The crossing tape bands from your third reference. Good energy, but it's a strong signature that would fight the stripe. Reserve for Direction C.

Cut

Spotlight cursor mask

Overused, invisible on touch, and it hides content — a straight Usability loss. Not worth it here.

Every one of these ships with a prefers-reduced-motion: reduce override that shows the final state. No exceptions — it's a scored criterion, not a courtesy.
07

The hero film

You want her portrait moving the way the reference clip moves. That's a motion-transfer job, not a text-to-video job — the reference becomes the driving clip and her photograph is the subject, so the framing, cardigan colour and studio backdrop all survive intact.

The route is Higgsfield's motion-control path: driving video in, still image in, animated subject out. It preserves identity far better than describing her in a prompt, which is the failure mode where the output stops looking like her.

Square, 1:1. One render serves both breakpoints — centred in a wide letterbox on desktop with the palette either side, full-bleed on mobile. It also halves the pixels of a 16:9 at the same visual height, which the performance budget likes.

Consent: given. Still outstanding — the original full-resolution portrait file. The version in chat is compressed, and motion transfer amplifies compression artefacts in the face first.

01

Asset spec

1:1 square, one render for both breakpoints. 6–8s, seamless loop. Frame 0 must already read as her portrait — that frame is the poster and the LCP element.

02

Prompt, shown to you first

Written out copy-paste ready before anything is spent. You approve the wording.

03

You pick model + preset

I list what's actually available on the account and you choose. I don't pick this for you.

04

Generate, then budget

Target under 2.5MB for the desktop loop, AV1/VP9 with an H.264 fallback, poster JPEG served immediately. Mobile gets the 9:16 or a still — never the 16:9 at full weight on cellular.
